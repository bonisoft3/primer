Told Apart — engineering design

Compiled from brief.md (pinned above) by the studio team, onto the mecha cluster, the omnishell terminal, the sayt loop and the bayt build graph.

Overview

Fourteen theme stylesheets, 959 declarations each, and one question asked of every semantic opposition in them: can the reader this theme is named for tell these two colours apart? Every entity takes the tab path (decision-01): nothing is published, no migration is emitted, and the whole data plane is the terminal's in-memory collections. Nine collections are seeded out of the vendored tarball and never written; five are written by two reduces at the mutation seat.

The load-bearing idea is that the arithmetic is implemented twice, over two data paths, and the two must agree. The app computes each separation in a Jessie reduce over rows in the tab (audit); tests/ground-truth.ts computes the same number in Deno over the vendored bytes — parsing the CSS, resolving aliases to a fixpoint within each theme, compositing alpha over that theme's own --bgColor-default, applying the Machado–Oliveira–Fernandes severity-1.0 matrix and taking OKLab ΔE. The two share no code (decision-03). Two implementations of colour-vision simulation agreeing is not the default state of the world.

The second half is the clamp. At integrate, Chromium reads getComputedStyle off both specimen elements of every graded opposition and the ΔE is recomputed FROM THE PAINTED COLOURS (decision-04). A blank page has no specimens; a transparent specimen matches nothing; a screen showing the wrong theme's row matches the wrong theme. No stylesheet in this app may hold a colour literal, so every specimen arrives as a bound inline custom property from a row — which is exactly what makes that check grade the data path.

The type-role finding, written from the EMITTED stylesheet at build time rather than authored (fixtures/typerungs.ts). Screens

roster — the fourteen, and what a pronto identity can hold

Reads: Theme (filter ground=eq.<ground>, order pos.asc) once per ground, so the fourteen read grouped without a region that can group; Score (filter theme=eq.<id>&reader=eq.<reader>) as a nested leaf slot on each row, which is what makes the roster's numbers the readings'; Cell (order id.asc) for the eighteen; and Theme twice more, filtered on wearable, for the twin closure and the two orphans.

Mutations: none. This is the one route on the strip and the app's only index, so the terminal draws no strip at all and every other screen carries its own one-link way back — the shape shadcnui settled. Seat A stands here as it does on every screen.

theme — one theme audited

Reads: Theme (filter id=eq.{param.theme}) as the root, so the title and the axes are the theme's own; Opposition (filter graded=eq.true, order pos.asc) for the blocks and again on graded=eq.false for the declined rows; Reading twice per block — once filtered painted=eq.yes for the specimens and once unfiltered on pos.asc for the four numbers; Reader (order pos.asc) for the picker.

Mutations: one form, four times over — the reader picker writes the one Cursor row (pick-reader). keep: 0: a parametrized route would otherwise accumulate one held screen per theme ever visited, and the colours on a held screen are the previous theme's.

opposition — one opposition, all fourteen themes

Reads: Opposition (filter id=eq.{param.opposition}) as the root; Segment (filter id=eq.{segment}) for what the opposition means; Reading (filter opposition=eq.{param.opposition}, order theme.asc,pos.asc) for the column.

Mutations: none. keep: 0, for the reason the audit declares it.

pairings — the pairing sheet

Reads: Theme (filter id=eq.{param.theme}) as the root; Floor (filter applies=eq.ratio, order pos.asc) for the two published thresholds; Ratio (filter theme=eq.{param.theme}, order pairing.asc) for the drawn rows; Pairing (order pos.asc) for the obligations themselves.

Mutations: none. Each row is DRAWN as the text it would actually be, through a bound inline custom property carrying the ground and the ink — the same channel every specimen takes, for the same reason.

tokens — the declarations

Reads: Theme (filter id=eq.{param.theme}) as the root; Census (order id.asc) at the head, each row a reduce over the rows below it; Token (filter theme=eq.{param.theme}, order name.asc) for every column the audit reads.

Mutations: none, and the screen offers none — nothing in this app writes to a token, a theme or a reading.

spec — the specification

Reads: Claim (order pos.asc) for the three columns; Reader and Floor (order pos.asc) for the method; Segment (order pos.asc) for the rule the pair roster is derived by; Census (order id.asc) for the count and the reduction of it.

Mutations: none. This screen is load-bearing for the whole app rather than an appendix to it: the app grades a vendor and disagrees with it in two places, and a missing citation would turn a finding into an accusation.

Handlers

audit — the app's own colour-vision arithmetic

Reads eleven collections and writes four. It carries its own sRGB-to-linear, its own Machado matrix multiply off the Reader rows, its own OKLab and its own WCAG relative luminance, and it shares not a line with tests/ground-truth.ts. The one quantity it does NOT compute is the compositing: it reads Token.composited, and the fixture check holds that column equal to the oracle's own compositing of the raw declaration. That is a stated departure, not an oversight — both routes read the ground from the same row, and a real page composites over whatever is behind.

focus — which reading the specimens wear

A separate seat, because it depends on the cursor and on nothing else: moving the reader repaints the specimens without re-folding a thousand separations. It owns Reading.painted and writes no other column.

Tests

test-fixture — Every seeded row against a SECOND parse of the vendored bytes. fixtures/generate.ts carries its own reader and tests/ground-truth.ts carries another, so this is a comparison of two parses and not of a value with itself. It also holds the app's fixture pin equal to the platform's source.json integrity, and hashes the quotation IN THE SUITE, so editing the fixture cannot move its own target.

test-separation — Mounts each theme's audit off the seed with no live service, reads every printed separation and verdict off the rendered text, and asserts equality with the ground truth. Then the per-theme verdict distribution, which is the anti-vacuity core: a suite asserting "all pass" is red on day one and one asserting "all fail" is red too. Then each of the four findings as a DIRECTION rather than a constant.

test-pairing — Every ratio against the ground truth, both threshold verdicts present on every row, and the dimmed ground's twelve muted misses named individually — a case that goes red if the app quietly picks a threshold.

test-census — The reader axis stops at the semantic layer. One measured fact, one refusal to grade, with the number as the reason.

test-coverage — Both anti-joins: every ground-truth reading is shown by some screen, and every number rendered on any screen is graded by some obligation. Each is driven to red on a doctored input by its own case — a screen with a number removed, a roster with a pair dropped — so a check that has stopped grading anything cannot stay green. It also runs every language finding's probe through cue and holds cue's answer to the reading beside it.

test-paint — The spine's clamp, and the only check that can see a blank page. One page.evaluate per screen collects getComputedStyle on both specimen elements of every graded opposition, and the ΔE is recomputed from those. It reads a PAINTED property, never the custom property, because getPropertyValue returns the unresolved two-argument form. Narrowing it to a sample of themes would put the spine back to being a fixture grade with a UI.

test-roster — The roster lands on the fourteen with their scores already computed, the four unpublished cells and two orphan themes are rows, and the mount makes no write to any seeded collection.

test-routes — Two addresses that name different themes never paint the same specimens — the check a keep: 1 parametrized route would fail while looking right.

Decisions

01 — everything is tab, and the grader is the reason. No Postgres, no Electric, no conduit, no bloblang, no migration, no policy, no publication, no pipeline. The reason is not the size: for an app whose oracle IS arithmetic, putting the arithmetic in a seat no fixture harness reaches destroys the grader before it is written. A reduce at the mutation seat is the one seat an off-disk mount executes. Nothing is at device either — a device collection persists across program versions and nothing backfills a new column, and this schema is certain to move.

02 — the seat is pinned on a row declared last. A data-on-mutation seat wakes when its OWN region's read changes and at no other time. The seed is written collection by collection when the tab store is first opened, so a seat pinned on Token would fold over a world with no Reader rows in it and produce nothing, with nothing red. Fold is declared last and carries one row, so the seat's wake is the last thing the seeding does.

03 — the arithmetic is written twice and the two files share nothing. shell/handlers/audit.js and tests/ground-truth.ts both implement sRGB-to-linear, the Machado matrix multiply, OKLab and the Euclidean distance, from the published model rather than from each other. A shared helper would turn the agreement into a tautology, and the agreement is the grade. The same reasoning splits fixtures/generate.ts from the oracle's parser: the fixture check compares two parses of the same bytes.

04 — the separation is not finished until a browser has painted it. The five checks that run at test mount into linkedom and compute no style, so none of them can tell a painted specimen from an empty box. paint reads getComputedStyle off the specimen elements over the running cluster and recomputes the ΔE from what was resolved. The dark half is reached the way the platform resolves appearance — through the context's colour scheme — never by toggling a class.

05 — the matrix is nine integer columns at ten-thousandths. A reduce has no matrix library, so the model has to arrive as data. Integers rather than floats because the two routes are held to agree EXACTLY at hundredths, and a float column would put a rounding argument between them. It also puts the model on the specification screen, where a reviewer can check that the app simulates what it says it simulates.

06 — a pair whose two members never differ is shown and not graded. Three of the twenty-two pairs the segment rule admits resolve to the same bytes in all fourteen themes: the diff-blob inks. The ground carries that opposition and the ink does not. Grading them would report a zero separation as a failure in every theme and say nothing about any of them, and dropping them silently would hide a fact about the system. They are rows with their reason on them.

07 — the simulated colours are columns on the reading, not a second set of rows. The reader control has to repaint the SAME elements — a second set of pre-simulated rows swapped in would make the simulation the fixture's and not the app's, and paint would be grading a transcription. So each reading carries the two colours as that reader sees them, computed by the app's own arithmetic, and the picker moves which reading is painted.

08 — the claim and the reading are different columns, and the probe is a third. says is the vendor's own sentence, quoted out of the vendored guide by a substring match that raises if the guide does not carry it. reads is what this app computes. They sit in adjacent cells and are never merged, so rewording the app's reading to match the specification would be a diff on a column that is supposed to be a quotation. probe is empty exactly where the claim is the vendor's, and carries the unification for every language finding — which is the difference between this and a hand-written table of things a vocabulary cannot say.

09 — the chart palette is counted and declined, with the number as the reason. Primer's seventeen categorical chart colours are constant within each ground across all fourteen themes, and their worst all-pairs separation is under a third of a unit for every reader. So the reader axis never reaches them, and grading a seventeen-slot categorical set would be vacuous in the other direction — the same failure in all fourteen themes. The app records the number rather than grading it.

10 — no verdict is carried by colour, and that is a consequence rather than a taste. The app's own audit found no theme in its subject that survives all three readers. A tool that encoded its answer in a hue would therefore be unreadable to one of the three readers it serves, and its own subject is the proof. Every verdict on every screen is a word, and paint asserts that no two verdict elements differ only in computed colour.
